💸 Step 2: Mapping Out the Money (The Unit Economics)
To make Sarig sustainable, you have to establish a clear flow of cash. The giant international apps operating in the Philippines (like GrabFood and Foodpanda) typically charge local restaurants a staggering 20% to 30% commission per order. This is where Sarig can win local loyalty by undercutting them.

Let's look at a concrete example using a highly competitive 15% Merchant Commission model on a standard order:

The Order: A customer orders a ₱200 meal.

The Delivery Fee: You set a flat rate of ₱45 based on Marawi's city limits.

Total Customer Payment: ₱245

Where does that ₱245 go?

The Merchant keeps ₱170: They get their ₱200 minus Sarig's 15% cut (₱30).

The Rider keeps ₱45: The rider keeps 100% of the delivery fee. (You want your riders happy so they don't jump ship to a competitor).

Sarig keeps ₱30: This is your gross profit. From this ₱30, you pay for your server costs, database operations, and marketing.

If you can process 100 orders a day at that average rate, Sarig generates ₱3,000 daily to keep the lights on and scale the business.

🛠️ Step 3: Deciding on the Technology (The Architecture)
The biggest trap founders fall into is thinking a food delivery platform is just one app. It is actually three distinct interfaces communicating with one central database in real-time.

To build Sarig, your system architecture will eventually need:

The Customer App (Mobile): The highly polished app where users browse menus, add items to a cart, and track their driver.

The Merchant App (Tablet/Web): A loud, simple interface sitting on a cafe counter. It rings when an order comes in, allows the kitchen to click "Preparing," and lets them mark items as "Sold Out."

The Rider App (Mobile): A GPS-enabled app where drivers can toggle themselves "Online," accept incoming pings, and map the route to the restaurant and customer.

The MVP Strategy (Minimum Viable Product):
Do not try to build three native mobile apps at the same time. That will take a year. For your Phase 1 launch, build a native mobile app for the Customers, but build the Merchant and Rider interfaces as simple, mobile-responsive web applications. It cuts your development time in half.
